How it is measured

Every tone figure on this site comes from one simple procedure, applied to headlines and nothing else. It is worth knowing exactly what it does, because it is easy to read more into a number like −0.33 than it can carry.

How one headline is scored

  1. The headline is lowercased and its accents folded, so Türkiye and Turkiye count as the same word.
  2. Its words are looked up in two lists: one of words that carry a negative judgement, one of positive. The lists hold 148 and 71 entries, in English, Spanish and German.
  3. The score is positives minus negatives, capped at ±3. Most headlines contain no listed word at all and score 0.
  4. A region's figure is the average over every headline sampled for it in the window.

A worked example

Turkish lira slumps as inflation fears mount−2
Exports rise and factory orders rebound+2
Central bank holds rates at 42 percent0

What the scale actually looks like

This is every measured headline in the current window, by score. The great majority sit at zero. That is the single most important thing to understand: a regional average of −0.33 does not mean the typical headline scores −0.33, it means the balance of the minority that do carry signal words leans negative.

What it cannot do

  • It does not read. Irony, quotation, negation and humour all fool it: no deaths reported scores negative.
  • It measures wording, not reality. A grim tone means the press wrote grimly, not that conditions are bad.
  • The word lists had to be audited. An early version scored Venezuela positive because one oil deal fired the word deal 415 times; transactional words were pruned out.
  • Headlines in languages outside English, Spanish and German score 0 and dilute their region's average.

Tone is the signal-word balance of headline wording, roughly −1 to +1. It measures how the press wrote, not what happened. How it is measured.
